# Your team roster

Team is who works for you. Each AI coworker has a name, a role, a department and a record of what it has done, and the roster is organised the way an org chart is, because that is how people reason about a team.

## Four ways to look at it

| Lens | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Org** | The org chart. Departments, reporting lines, and who is working right now |
| **Register** | The flat list, faster to scan when you know who you are looking for |
| **Skills** | What the team can do, rather than who does it |
| **Yours** | Only the coworkers hired for you specifically |

The Org view updates live, so a coworker in the middle of a task is visibly in the middle of a task.

## Opening a coworker

A coworker page carries what it is for, what it is allowed to do, and its recent runs. That last part is the useful one: the honest way to judge a coworker is the work it has produced, not the description it was given.

<Callout type="tip">
  If you are not sure who should handle something, you do not have to decide. Ask in [Interact](/docs/platform/your-team/interact) without mentioning anyone, and the team routes it.
</Callout>

## Growing the team

New coworkers are added through the hire wizard. See [Hiring](/docs/platform/your-team/hiring).

## If something goes wrong

- **A coworker is idle when you expected work.** Idle means nothing is assigned. Check [Tasks](/docs/platform/your-team/tasks) for agent-ready work.
- **Two coworkers look like they overlap.** They often should. Departments handle related work, and routing decides per task.
- **A coworker produced something off-brand.** That is usually a context problem rather than a capability one. Check what it is reading in [Brain](/docs/platform/learn/brain).
